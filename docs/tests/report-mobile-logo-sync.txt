## What was wrong, measured

Colour profiles of the shipped assets on `main`:

    icon.png                    27.1% transparent, dominant #000020 (dark navy plate)
    splash-icon.png             27.2% transparent, dominant #000020   ← same artwork
    favicon.png                 24.9% transparent, dominant #000020   ← same artwork
    android-icon-foreground.png 98.2% transparent, dominant #20c0e0   ← the OLD cyan hub
    android-icon-monochrome.png 98.2% transparent, same silhouette    ← from the OLD mark
    android-icon-background.png 100% opaque #000000

So #50 replaced the brand icon, the favicon and the splash, and left the three
Android adaptive assets on the previous mark. Nothing failed at any gate,
because **nothing tied the derived icons to the source they are derived from**.

## Extracting the mark

Colour distance does not separate mark from plate: the plate is a gradient (the
three corner samples were `#12296b`, `#012f75`, `#000f37`) and its rounded
outline is as saturated as the mark. Thresholding by luminance alone kept that
outline, and because the outline reaches further than the mark it also drove the
centring and the scale — the mark came out small and off-centre.

Luminance plus connected components separates them cleanly:

    largest components (px): 275511, 9710, 46, 41
                             ^mark   ^plate outline

Keeping only the largest leaves the mark with its glow, no plate edge.

Scale then follows geometry instead of a guessed number: the mark is fitted so
its outermost visible pixel lands exactly on the 66% safe circle — the region
Android guarantees survives every launcher mask.

    significant radius 337px / safe radius 338px
    background colour sampled from the plate: #011133

`app.json`'s `adaptiveIcon.backgroundColor` moves from `#0b0b0d` to that sampled
colour. Left at the old value, any launcher mask larger than the background
image would show a ring of the wrong dark around the icon.

## Revision 2: a recorded process, not a recorded result

Revision 1 generated the layers on a laptop with Pillow and SciPy — neither is a
dependency of this repository — and recorded digests. Two problems with that:

1. **It recorded what the files are, not how they came to be.** Anything the
   build cannot re-run is a claim.
2. **Digests do not catch the mistake that shipped.** Replace the brand mark and
   leave the derived layers untouched, and every derived digest still matches,
   because those files were never edited. Only the source digest moves — and
   updating it is the obvious thing to do when you change the source.

`scripts/generate-android-icons.mjs` now carries the process end to end, using
one declared dependency (`pngjs`) and arithmetic: luminance ramp, largest
connected component, area-average resample, safe-circle fit, and the background
colour as the per-channel median of the plate pixels — a rule that re-derives
itself on new artwork rather than a number someone read once.

The test re-runs it into a temporary directory and compares **decoded pixels**
against what is committed. Pixels rather than bytes, so a different PNG encoder
is not mistaken for a different icon.

Cross-checks before committing:

- consecutive runs are byte-identical;
- the JS pipeline finds the same 275511-pixel component and the same `#011133`
  plate colour as the throwaway Python one it replaces;
- the manifest records the generator, its digest, every parameter and the colour
  rule, so a silent change to the process is as visible as one to its output.

## The guard

`assets/icon-derivation.json` records the source digest, the three derived
digests, the canvas, the safe-zone fraction and the sampled colour.
`src/icon-assets.test.ts` recomputes all of it.

The important assertion is the first one: **the brand source must still hash to
what the icons were derived from**. That is exactly the invariant whose absence
allowed this bug — updating `icon.png` without regenerating now fails at commit
time rather than being noticed in a screenshot after release.

## Verified elsewhere, and what is still open

`splash-icon.png` and `favicon.png` were checked and are **already** the current
artwork — their colour profiles match `icon.png`. They are not touched here.

iOS `AppIcon` is generated by `expo prebuild` from `expo.icon`, which points at

proof.
